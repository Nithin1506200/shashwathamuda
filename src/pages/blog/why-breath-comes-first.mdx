Most people arrive at yoga through the body. A stiff back, a restless mind, a doctor's suggestion. The tradition, however, has always placed the **breath** at the centre. In the *Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā*, the movement of breath and the movement of mind are described as inseparable: steady one, and the other follows.

## The breath as a bridge

The breath is the only vital function that is both automatic and voluntary. It runs on its own while we sleep, yet we can slow, lengthen or pause it at will. This makes it a bridge between the involuntary body and the deliberate mind, and it is why pranayama sits between asana and meditation in the classical eight limbs.

<Callout title="A five-minute beginning">
Sit comfortably. Let the exhale be a little longer than the inhale, counting four in and six out. Do this for five minutes, every morning, for a week. Notice what changes.
</Callout>

## What we teach first

In our regular batches, the first four weeks include:

1. Observing the natural breath without changing it
2. Diaphragmatic (abdominal) breathing lying down
3. Lengthening the exhale
4. Simple alternate-nostril breathing without retention

Only after these are comfortable do we introduce ratios and retention. Going slowly is not a compromise. It is the method.

> "When the breath wanders the mind also is unsteady. But when the breath is calmed the mind too will be still." — Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā II.2
