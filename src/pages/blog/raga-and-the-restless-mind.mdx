In Indian thought, sound is not merely heard. *Nāda* is described as the subtle vibration that underlies both the outer world and the inner. The Veena, with its long sustain and gentle gamakas, is one of the most direct ways to experience this.

## Listening as practice

A raga-based relaxation session at Shashwatamuda looks simple from the outside: dim lights, a Veena, a few instructions on breathing, and about forty minutes of listening. Inside, something shifts. Participants often describe a slowing of thought and a sense of spaciousness.

We do not make medical claims about this. What we can say is that attentive listening, combined with slow breathing, is a reliable way to settle the nervous system for many people.

<Callout title="Try this at home">
Choose a single alapana recording of about ten minutes in a calming raga such as *Hamsadhwani* or *Kalyani*. Sit or lie down, close your eyes, and follow only the melody. When the mind wanders, return to the sound.
</Callout>

## Why ragas and not playlists?

A raga is a living framework rather than a fixed tune. Because the musician unfolds it slowly, the listener is invited to stay with one mood for a long time. That sustained attention is where the practice lies.
