Yoga therapy has grown quickly as a field, and so has the research. As an initiative that runs therapeutic programs, we think it is important to be honest about what the evidence supports.

## Where the evidence is relatively strong

- **Chronic low back pain.** Several reviews find yoga comparable to other forms of exercise for reducing pain and improving function.
- **Stress and anxiety symptoms.** Breath-based and mindfulness-based yoga interventions show consistent, moderate benefits in many trials.
- **Sleep quality.** Gentle evening practice and pranayama are associated with improved self-reported sleep.

## Where it is promising but not settled

Areas such as metabolic health, hypertension and mood disorders have encouraging studies, but many are small, short or lack strong control groups. Larger, well-designed trials are still needed.

<Callout title="Our position">
Yoga therapy at Shashwatamuda is offered as a complementary, educational practice. It is never a replacement for medical diagnosis or treatment, and we ask participants to keep their healthcare providers informed.
</Callout>

## How we use research in our programs

Every therapeutic module we design starts from a literature review, is written as a structured protocol, and is reviewed with our integrative medicine advisors. We also collect anonymised feedback so that programs improve over time.
