**Shāśvata** (शाश्वत) means eternal, timeless, enduring. **Muda** (मुद) means joy, delight, gladness. Placed together, *Shashwatamuda* points to a joy that does not depend on circumstances.

## Not happiness, not pleasure

The Bharatiya Shāstras draw a careful distinction between *sukha* (pleasant experience), *ānanda* (bliss as one's own nature) and *muda* (delight). Pleasure comes and goes with its objects. Muda, as the tradition uses it, is closer to the quiet gladness of a mind that is settled and clear.

## Why this is the name

Yoga and music are two of the most reliable ways human beings have found to reach that settled clarity. Yoga works through body and breath; music works through sound and attention. Bharatiya knowledge systems give both a philosophical home. Research helps us understand and communicate their effects. Seva turns the whole thing outward.

That is the architecture of Shashwatamuda, and it is why we describe ourselves with one line:

> Ancient Wisdom. Contemporary Well-being. Eternal Joy.
